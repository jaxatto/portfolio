# Portfolio critique

Audience: a hiring manager or design leader skimming for 2 minutes, then a few reading closely. Role: staff-level product designer. Be direct, specific and kind. Don't pad with praise, and don't rewrite silently; propose and discuss (D-004).

For the page or section under review, check:

1. **Clarity in 5 seconds.** Who is this, what do they do, why should I care? Is the headline concrete rather than a generic title?
2. **Evidence of impact.** Outcomes, metrics, scope, scale, who was affected. Flag claims with no result and ask Jax for the real numbers instead of inventing any.
3. **Staff-level signals.** Strategy and judgment (why this problem, what was traded off), influence across teams, systems thinking, mentoring/leadership, not just execution.
4. **Jax's role.** What did *Jax* do versus the team? Is it clear and honest?
5. **Story shape.** Problem → approach → decisions → result → reflection. Cut process that doesn't support the point; keep it skimmable (headings, short paragraphs, pull-out results).
6. **Voice.** Plain, confident, specific. Flag buzzwords, hedging, passive voice, and anything that reads like a job description.
7. **Visual and interaction craft.** Hierarchy, spacing rhythm, consistency with tokens/components, image quality and captions, empty/hover/focus states, responsive behavior.
8. **Accessibility** (a core part of Jax's pitch): contrast, heading order, alt text, keyboard, motion. The site itself should demonstrate the skill.
9. **Trust and polish.** Typos, broken links, stale dates, placeholder content, load speed, a working resume link, clear contact path.

Output: a short list ordered by impact on getting hired. For each item give what you saw, why it matters, and a concrete proposed change (draft copy or a specific design adjustment) that Jax can accept, tweak or reject. Separate "quick wins" from "bigger changes". End with the 1–3 questions whose answers would most improve the page (for example, missing metrics).
